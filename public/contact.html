Contact

The best way to reach us is by email: info@pondco.online. We read everything and reply to substantive inquiries as promptly as we can.

What to send us

Corrections. If you find an error in our material — a wrong term, an outdated process description, a broken reference — tell us exactly which page and which passage is wrong, and what the correct information is. Corrections backed by a primary source (an agency publication, a standard, an official record) are the fastest to verify and fix.

Topic suggestions. We prioritize topics that help readers evaluate real aviation-facility projects: planning, scoping, delivery methods, funding, construction practice, and verification of project claims. Tell us what you are trying to understand and why; the more specific the suggestion, the more useful it is.

Source pointers. If you know of a public record, agency guide, or dataset that our readers should know about, send the link with a sentence about what it covers. We regularly fold reader-suggested sources into our articles.

Partnership and enterprise inquiries. For questions about enterprise use, partnerships, or technical matters regarding the site, use the same address with a clear subject line describing the inquiry.

What we cannot do

We cannot provide engineering, legal, procurement, or safety advice about a specific project — by email or otherwise. If your question is really "what should we do about our runway/taxiway/terminal," you need the licensed professionals and authorities with jurisdiction over your facility, not a knowledge base. We will politely decline and suggest where to look instead.

We also cannot confirm or investigate claims about specific active projects from email alone; verification requires access to the primary records, which we describe in our guide to evaluating project information.

Before you write

Many questions are already answered on the site. The FAQ covers common questions about airport planning, funding, and project delivery; the topic pages explain the phases of a project; and the insights articles go deep on subjects like delivery methods, grant funding, and commissioning. A quick search of the site first will usually get you an answer faster than an email exchange.

Research and media inquiries

If you are a researcher, student, or journalist working with our material, you are welcome to cite it with attribution and a link. For questions about our sources or methodology behind a specific article, include the article title and the passage in question — that lets us give you a precise answer rather than a general one. We cannot provide interviews on short deadlines, but we do respond to substantive written questions.

What happens after you send a message

Substantive messages are read by the editorial side of the operation. Corrections are verified against primary sources before any change is published; if we cannot verify a claimed error, we will tell you what we found rather than making the change blindly. Topic suggestions are logged and weighed against our editorial priorities — we cannot respond to every suggestion individually, but the good ones shape the publishing calendar. Partnership inquiries receive a reply if there is a fit; unsolicited sales pitches do not.

Response times

We aim to acknowledge substantive messages within a few business days. Correction reports are triaged first. We do not operate a phone line or a public office for site inquiries; email is the channel that reaches the editorial side of the operation.

Privacy note

If you email us, we will use your message and address only to respond and, where relevant, to improve the site. We do not sell contact information or add correspondents to marketing lists. See the privacy policy for details.
