About Us

Pondco Online is an independent information resource publishing practical, plainly written background material on aviation-facility planning and delivery. It exists for people who need clear context on complex airport projects: airport staff evaluating a capital program, consultants and contractors getting oriented, students learning how public infrastructure works, and members of the public trying to understand a project in their community.

What we publish

Our material covers the full arc of an aviation-facility project: defining the operating problem, scoping the work, feasibility and planning, design coordination across disciplines, procurement and delivery methods, construction on active airfields, commissioning, and closeout. We also publish guidance on the surrounding context — public accountability, stakeholder coordination, funding programs, and how to verify project claims against primary sources.

Everything here is written to be read by non-specialists without dumbing down the subject. We explain terms when they first appear, we show how the pieces connect, and we point readers toward the authoritative records — owner documents, procurement notices, permits, and agency publications — that real decisions are based on.

What we are not

Pondco Online is an educational publisher, not an engineering firm, law firm, or procurement advisor. Nothing on this site is professional advice. Decisions affecting an active facility should be made with the appropriate licensed professionals and the public authorities that have jurisdiction. We say this on every page because it matters: background knowledge helps you ask better questions, but it does not replace qualified judgment applied to your specific situation.

We are also not a news outlet. We do not report on individual projects as they happen, and we do not publish project announcements, rankings, or endorsements. Our focus is durable background knowledge — the kind of material that stays useful regardless of which project is in the headlines.

How the material is produced

Articles are researched from public records, agency guidance, and established industry practice, then written in plain language and reviewed for accuracy before publication. When we describe a process — how grant funding works, how a delivery method distributes risk, how commissioning is structured — we describe the general pattern, note where practice varies by jurisdiction, and direct readers to the governing documents for their specific case.

We correct errors when we find them. If you spot something wrong or outdated, contact us and we will investigate and fix it.

Operation

Pondco Online is operated by 3000 Studios with supporting delivery partners. Editorial decisions — what to publish, how to frame it, and when to correct it — are made independently of any advertising on the site. Advertising, where present, is served by third-party networks and does not influence content.

Editorial standards

Every article states its limits. When a process varies by jurisdiction — procurement rules, permit timelines, funding eligibility — we say so rather than presenting one state's practice as universal. When a number is an industry rule of thumb rather than a measured value, we label it as such. And when we do not know something, we say that too, and point to where the answer can be found. A knowledge base earns trust one accurate, carefully bounded claim at a time.

How to use the knowledge base

Start with the topic pages if you are new to airport projects — they lay out the phases and the vocabulary. Use the insights articles when you need depth on a specific subject, from reading a master plan to managing closeout. Use the FAQ for quick answers, and the guide to evaluating project information whenever you need to check a claim against the record. The material is organized so that each piece stands alone but the whole forms a coherent introduction to how aviation facilities get planned and built.
